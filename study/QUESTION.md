# KILL-SWITCH-REFERENCE-001 — QUESTION

Can we construct a bounded reference architecture in which:

1. multiple workers/receivers may already be running;
2. some receivers may have stale local knowledge;
3. work may have been admitted before STOP;
4. credentials/artifacts may remain authentic;
5. workers themselves may remain online and capable;

BUT:

once the owner-controlled STOP becomes EFFECTIVE,

NO COVERED CONSEQUENTIAL EFFECT can commit using authority that existed before that STOP?

And can an independent audit process prove, path by path, what actually happened — without treating silence as success?

## Non-goals stated up front

This is not a button that stops a model process. It is a control that makes
previously authorized consequential work unable to cross its covered
consequence boundaries after STOP becomes effective. A kill switch cannot
undo a consequence that became irreversible before STOP. No retroactive
magic is promised or tested.
