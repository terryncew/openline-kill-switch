Show HN: A reference kill switch for AI consequences (not for models)

Most kill-switch discussion is about turning off the model. I built a
small reference that does something narrower and, I think, more precise:
after an owner-controlled STOP, previously issued authority can no longer
commit covered consequential effects — even through a stale receiver that
never got the shutdown message, or work that was already underway.

The agents keep running. Their old authority stops working at the final
consequence boundary, which serializes every effect commit against the
STOP's linearization point. A separate auditor re-derives every verdict
from hash-chained evidence; silence is never counted as success.

7/7 frozen post-stop survival paths STOPPED, 20 bounded STOP-vs-finalize
races with 0 forbidden orderings, fail-closed on unavailable authority,
restart-safe. Stdlib-only Python, two commands to reproduce:

./RUN.sh && ./VERIFY.sh

Exact earned claim and five hard limits in the repo. Reference
implementation, not production, not compliance. Would value hostile
reading of the threat model.
